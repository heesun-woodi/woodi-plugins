# Analysis-to-decision patterns (Phase 4)

The survey was built backward from a decision. Analysis closes the loop: turn tagged items back
into the decision. Because every item carries an `→RQ#` tag, analysis is mechanical — group by
tag, apply the move, fill the decision bridge.

## Analysis plan table

One row per RQ. Name the load-bearing (closed-form) item, the explaining (open-ended) item, and
the move.

| RQ | Load-bearing item(s) | Explaining item | Analysis move |
|---|---|---|---|
| RQ1 appeal | B1 Likert battery, B2 forced-rank | B3 open "ideal day" | Top-box (4–5) % → rank appeal points → headline candidates |
| RQ3 barriers | D1 single-choice, C2 concern battery | D2 open "what would reassure" | Rank barrier strength → items to pre-empt in messaging |

Order: use closed-form to find the pattern (counts, ranks, top-box %), then read the paired
open-ended answers to explain *why* the pattern looks the way it does.

## Conversion cross-tabs (when the goal is sign-ups / purchase / retention)

The single most valuable analysis for a conversion goal is **appeal × intent**: cross the
respondent's #1 appeal point (closed-form) against their stated intent (E-section scale). This
answers the question the whole survey exists for — *which appeal actually drives the action*, not
merely which is most liked. A point can be widely liked yet weakly convert; only the cross-tab
separates them.

Other high-value cross-tabs:
- **segment × barrier** (e.g. 맞벌이 여부 × 참여부담 우려) — does a sub-audience need its own message?
- **awareness × appeal** — what pulls people in *before* they understand the offer = the hook.

## Decision bridge

The payoff table. Left column = finding; right column = the action it triggers. This is what the
user actually walks away with.

| Finding (from analysis) | Action (the decision) |
|---|---|
| Appeal point X ranks #1 on top-box AND has highest intent cross-tab | Lead the campaign / product with X |
| Barrier Y is the top single-choice blocker | Pre-empt Y explicitly in messaging / FAQ / onboarding |
| Segment Z shows a distinct barrier profile | Create a Z-specific message variant |

For a "what to promote / what to build / what to fix" decision, the bridge is the deliverable —
the ranked findings map one-to-one onto ranked actions.

## When the survey feeds a message/offer (common for PM + growth work)

Add a **finding → hook** mapping: each winning appeal point paired with the specific product/
service strength that delivers it, and a one-line copy hook. Flag any right-column cell you had
to assume rather than know — the user often needs to supply the real differentiators, and it's
better to mark the gap than to invent strengths.
