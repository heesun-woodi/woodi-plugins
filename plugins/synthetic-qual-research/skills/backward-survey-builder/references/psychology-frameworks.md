# Optional: product-psychology item patterns

Backward design decides *what* to ask. These frameworks help decide *how to shape* items when
the goal is behavioral — conversion, motivation, habit, retention — rather than purely
informational. They are enhancers, not requirements. Reach for one when it earns its keep;
don't bolt all five onto every survey.

## BMAP — Behavior = Motivation × Ability × Prompt

A behavior happens only when motivation, ability, and a prompt coincide. Use it as a **coverage
checklist** so a survey about "why don't they act?" doesn't measure only motivation:

- **Motivation** items — emotional pulls: anticipation, sensation, belonging. ("이 서비스를 쓰면
  어떤 기분일 것 같나요?")
- **Ability** items — perceived barriers: time, money, physical/cognitive effort, skill. These
  are the "안 한 게 아니라 못 한다고 느낀" factors. Measure barrier *strength* on a scale.
- **Prompt** items — what/where/when would trigger the action (channel, moment, reminder).

If your matrix has motivation RQs but no ability RQs, BMAP is telling you the survey can't
explain a motivation–action gap. Add a barriers battery.

## B.I.A.S — a 4-stage filter for finding service gaps

Design questions that probe each stage a user's mind passes through:

- **Block** — does the value break through attention? (unaided recall, first impression)
- **Interpret** — is the value understood quickly and positively? (comprehension, association)
- **Act** — is the friction to act low? (barrier items, default-choice reactions)
- **Store** — does a positive memory form? (post-experience satisfaction, would-recommend)

Useful for structuring a full-journey survey: one small battery per stage.

## Peak-End — measure the peak and the end, not the average

People remember a peak moment and the ending, not the mean. Items that ask "가장 강렬했던
순간" and "마지막에 어떤 느낌으로 끝났나" surface more decision-relevant signal than "전반적으로
만족하셨나요?". Also useful: an open-ended "가장 막막했던/짜증났던 순간" catches the low point
that a satisfaction average hides.

## 6P storyboard — get the respondent to narrate the happy ending

Ask the respondent to picture the best-case outcome ("3년 뒤 …이 어떤 모습이면 '잘했다' 싶을까
요?"). Their answer is the aspiration in their own words — precisely the language that later
converts. This is a single powerful open-ended item, not a battery.

## Loss aversion — frame commitment asks as avoiding a loss

For the conversion/commitment item at the end, "놓치지 않도록 우선 안내를 받으시겠어요?" (avoid
missing out) tends to outperform "안내를 받으시겠어요?" (gain). Offer a soft middle option
("아직 고민 중 — 정보만") so hesitant respondents still convert to a lead instead of bouncing to
a hard no. Note: with synthetic respondents this measures *relative* framing effects reliably,
but treat absolute conversion rates as directional, not literal.
